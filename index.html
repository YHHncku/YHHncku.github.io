01 / Research

Design, cognition, and intelligent systems.

Our research explores how designed experiences influence human cognition and behavior, and how these insights can be used to create interactions that serve different human needs and goals.

Research Question

How does design shape what people think, feel, and do—often without them noticing?

Our work starts with people: understanding how they perceive information, make decisions, interact with technology, and respond to different forms of designed experiences.

We use design, empirical research, and experimentation to develop and evaluate new ways of interacting with intelligent and complex systems.

01

Information Visualization

Designing visual representations and interactive interfaces that help people understand complex information, interpret data, and make sense of AI-generated content.

02

Human-Centered AI

Exploring how AI systems can be designed around human needs, cognition, expectations, and decision-making.

03

Cognitive Engineering

Applying knowledge of human cognition and behavior to the design and evaluation of complex interactive systems.

02 / Current Research

Current research projects.

Our current work focuses on visualization, human-AI interaction, trust, emotion, and the design of AI-mediated experiences.

01

Designing Visualization-Based Interaction Interfaces for Human-AI Trust Calibration

This project explores how visualization-based interaction interfaces can help users better understand AI systems and calibrate their trust in AI-generated information and recommendations.

NSTC-funded Research 2025–2027
02

Mitigating Sycophantic Bias in AI Applications through Emotion-Adaptive Visualization Interfaces

This project investigates how emotion-adaptive visualization interfaces can be used to mitigate sycophantic bias in AI applications and support more effective human-AI interaction.

NSTC-funded Research 2026–2027
03 / About

A background across design, human factors, cognition, and AI.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Industrial Design at National Cheng Kung University.

My background bridges industrial design, human factors, cognitive science, and artificial intelligence. I received my Ph.D. in Industrial Engineering with a focus on Human Factors from Purdue University, following an M.S. and B.S. in Industrial Design from National Cheng Kung University.

Before joining NCKU, I worked as a Principal Engineer in the AI Solution Division at TSMC, where I worked on AI/ML solutions for semiconductor manufacturing. I previously spent four years as a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Department of Psychological Science at Purdue University.

This interdisciplinary background shapes how I approach research: starting with people, understanding how they think and behave, and using design and technology to create better experiences.

06 / Join the Lab

Curious about people, design, and AI?

I am interested in working with students who are curious about people, design, and emerging technologies.

Students with backgrounds or interests in industrial design, HCI, human factors, cognitive science, AI, or related fields are welcome to explore research opportunities.

Our research involves a combination of design, empirical research, experimentation, and prototyping. Students are encouraged to develop their own research questions and pursue them through rigorous investigation.
